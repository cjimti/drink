Rye, stirred

Vieux Carré

Q,Q,Q,1b,1,1,Rl

  • 3/4 oz Rye
  • 3/4 oz Cognac
  • 3/4 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 1 barspoon Bénédictine
  • 1 dash Angostura bitters
  • 1 dash Peychaud's bitters

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Rocks, iced, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Three-quarters of an ounce each of rye, cognac, and sweet vermouth, equal and small. One barspoon of Benedictine is honey and herbs. One dash of Angostura, one of Peychaud's. Rocks with ice, lemon. Split-base Manhattan with an herbal film.

History

Walter Bergeron, head bartender at the Hotel Monteleone in New Orleans, built the Vieux Carré in the 1930s and named it for the French Quarter, the old square. Stanley Clisby Arthur prints it in 1937: equal rye, cognac, and Italian vermouth, a half teaspoon of Benedictine, a dash each of Peychaud's and Angostura. The Carousel Bar, where the drink now lives, opened in 1949; Bergeron was mixing it in the hotel's earlier lounge. Chuck Taggart, in The Oxford Companion, notes Bergeron had worked the bar before Prohibition and returned at Repeal. This menu keeps the equal three-quarters and both bitters, with a barspoon of Benedictine, over ice with a lemon twist.
