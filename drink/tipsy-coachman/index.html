Vodka, stirred

Tipsy Coachman

1h,Q,1,Rl

  • 1 1/2 oz Vodka
  • 3/4 oz Bénédictine
  • 1 dash Angostura bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Rocks, iced, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of vodka, three-quarters of Bénédictine, one dash of Angostura. Honey and herbs, vodka as the thinner so the liqueur is a third of the drink. Stirred, rocks with ice, lemon twist. It sits, herbal and sweet, with bark at the end.

History

The formula is the Gypsy Queen: vodka, Bénédictine, and bitters. David Wondrich found the earliest printing in a 1938 booklet from New York's Russian Tea Room, one of the first American vodka cocktails. David Embury reprints it in 1948 as Gypsy or Gypsy Queen and prefers a drier ratio than the original one-to-two. A drink called Tipsy Coachman does not appear under that name in those books; the phrase is older, from Oliver Goldsmith, and later a legal doctrine. This menu keeps the ounce and a half of vodka, three-quarters of Bénédictine, and a dash of Angostura, over ice with a lemon twist.
