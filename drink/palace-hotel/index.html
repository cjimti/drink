Cognac, stirred

Palace Hotel

1h,1h,1b,cl

  • 1 1/2 oz Cognac
  • 1 1/2 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 1 barspoon Orange liqueur

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Coupe, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces and a half of cognac and sweet vermouth, grape on grape, a Manhattan ratio. One barspoon of orange liqueur is peel. No bitters. Stirred, coupe, lemon twist. A brandy Manhattan with a thin orange film.

History

A drink called Palace Hotel does not appear in the pre-1970 manuals under that name, nor in Difford's, Punch, or Imbibe. The nearest parent is the Harvard, which George J. Kappeler prints in 1895 Modern American Drinks: equal brandy and Italian vermouth, gum, Angostura, and a seltzer top. Albert Stevens Crockett, in Old Waldorf Bar Days (1931), ties the name to the college on the Charles. This menu keeps the equal cognac and sweet vermouth and the lemon twist, yet it drops the bitters and the soda, and puts a barspoon of orange liqueur in their place.
