Rye, stirred

Monte Carlo

1Q,h,1,R

  • 1 3/4 oz Rye
  • 1/2 oz Bénédictine
  • 1 dash Angostura bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Rocks, iced.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and three-quarters of rye against a half ounce of Benedictine. Honey, baking spice, and herbs ride on the whiskey, and one dash of Angostura is bark at the end. Rocks with ice. It sits.

History

The Monte Carlo first prints in David Embury's 1948 The Fine Art of Mixing Drinks: one part Benedictine, two parts rye, one or two dashes of Angostura, shaken with cracked ice. Embury, a New York tax lawyer, called it a bit on the sweet side and suggested lemon juice and more rye. No bartender or bar is named. The drink is a Manhattan with Benedictine in place of vermouth, served here over ice. This menu pours an ounce and three-quarters of rye, a half ounce of Benedictine, and one dash of Angostura.
