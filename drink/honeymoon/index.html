Apple Brandy, shaken

Honeymoon

Barline 1q,1,1b,h,1,Ro

  • 1 1/4 oz Apple brandy
  • 1 oz Bénédictine
  • 1 barspoon Orange liqueur
  • 1/2 oz Lemon juice
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Rocks, iced, orange twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a quarter of apple brandy against a full ounce of Benedictine: herb almost even with orchard. A barspoon of orange liqueur and a dash of orange bitters are peel. Half an ounce of lemon lifts it. Rocks, ice, orange twist: it sits and the Benedictine warms.

History

Hugo R. Ensslin first prints the Honey Moon Cocktail in Recipes for Mixed Drinks (1916): equal Benedictine and apple brandy, juice of half a lemon, three dashes of curacao. Ensslin was at the Hotel Wallick in Times Square. Craddock copies it into the Savoy in 1930. It later turns up at the Brown Derby in Los Angeles, and David Embury (1948) also calls it the Farmer's Daughter, with a much drier applejack-forward ratio. This menu pours an ounce and a quarter of apple brandy, a full ounce of Benedictine, a barspoon of orange liqueur, half an ounce of lemon, and a dash of orange bitters, over ice with an orange twist.
