Duplex

1h,1h,2,co

  • 1 1/2 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 1 1/2 oz Dry vermouth
  • 2 dashes Orange bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, orange twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces and a half of sweet and dry vermouth, so neither wins. Two dashes of orange bitters are peel on the split wine. Stirred, no spirit, coupe, orange twist. Grape and spice, cold, with a thin orange film on the wine.

History

Albert Stevens Crockett, in Old Waldorf Bar Days (1931), lists the Duplex as equal Italian and French vermouth with two dashes of orange bitters or two squeezes of orange peel, and calls it parent of the original Bronx. Johnnie Solon of the Waldorf said he was making a Duplex when Traverson, head waiter of the Empire Room, challenged him to invent a new cocktail, and he added gin and orange juice. This menu is the Waldorf Duplex: an ounce and a half of each vermouth, two dashes of orange bitters, stirred, coupe, orange twist.
