Cognac, shaken

Sidecar

Barline 1h,1,Q,cs

  • 1 1/2 oz Cognac
  • 1 oz Orange liqueur
  • 3/4 oz Lemon juice

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Coupe, sugar rim.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of cognac, a full ounce of orange liqueur, three-quarters lemon. The orange liqueur is heavier than the lemon, so the drink is peel and candy. Shaken, coupe, sugar rim.

History

Robert Vermeire prints the Sidecar in May 1922 in Cocktails How to Mix Them: equal cognac, Cointreau, and lemon, very popular in France, and first introduced in London by Pat MacGarry of Buck's Club. Harry MacElhone, who had worked with MacGarry, prints the same formula later that year and later takes the credit himself. Frank Meier at the Ritz Bar in Paris listed it among cocktails new since the war. David Embury, in 1948, credits an unnamed American captain in a Paris sidecar. The origin stays disputed. This menu pours an ounce and a half of cognac, a full ounce of orange liqueur, and three-quarters lemon, shaken, with a sugar rim.
