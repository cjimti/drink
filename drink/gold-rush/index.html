Taste

Two ounces of bourbon, three-quarters lemon, three-quarters honey syrup. Honey is thick and floral against the lemon. Shaken, rocks with ice. Bourbon, citrus, and a brown pour that sits.

History

T.J. Siegal, a childhood friend of Sasha Petraske, asked for honey syrup in his bourbon sour at Milk & Honey in New York around 2000, shortly after Petraske opened the bar on New Year's Eve 1999. Siegal named it while drinking the first one. Robert Simonson traces the story in Punch; Sam Ross later built the Penicillin from the same honey-sour idea. This menu keeps the two ounces of bourbon and three-quarters each of lemon and honey syrup, over ice. No twist.
