Taste

Two ounces of bourbon and soda over ice, with a lemon twist. Water and gas open the whiskey out, so corn and barrel come forward and the vanilla rides up on the bubbles. The twist puts oil on the surface, so the first sip is citrus over sweet grain.

History

The word turns up in the 1890s. A character asks for a high ball of whiskey in Ha Du Souchet's 1894 play My Friend From India, and Chris Lawlor's 1895 The Mixicologist prints High Ball as ice, seltzer, and brandy or whiskey; Harry Johnson's 1900 Bartenders Manual settles it as one word. Gary Regan offered a different etymology in The Joy of Mixology (2003): a highball was a railroad signal, the ball on a float in a steam engine's water tank showing there was water enough to go. This menu pours two ounces of bourbon over ice, tops with soda, and twists lemon over it.
