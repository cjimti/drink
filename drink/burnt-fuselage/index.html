Cognac, stirred

Burnt Fuselage

1,1,1,c

  • 1 oz Cognac
  • 1 oz Dry vermouth
  • 1 oz Orange liqueur

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces of cognac, dry vermouth, and orange liqueur, so the orange is a third of the drink. Candied peel sits against the cognac; dry vermouth cuts the sugar. Stirred, no bitters, coupe, no twist. A stiff orange Manhattan, grape and peel.

History

The Burnt Fuselage first appears in Harry MacElhone's 1927 Barflies and Cocktails, in the Cocktails Around Town section listing drinks of regulars at Harry's New York Bar in Paris. MacElhone credits Colonel C.W. Chuck Kerwood of Philadelphia, President of the International League of Aviators, who wanted a stiff steadier when he came down to earth: one-third Grand Marnier, one-third cognac, and one-third French vermouth. Kerwood was known as the wild man of aviation. This menu keeps the equal parts and the coupe, with no bitters and no twist.
