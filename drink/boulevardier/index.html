Bourbon, stirred

Boulevardier

1h,1,1,R

  • 1 1/2 oz Bourbon
  • 1 oz Campari
  • 1 oz Sweet vermouth

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Rocks, iced.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of bourbon against a full ounce each of Campari and sweet vermouth. The whiskey leads a little; Campari is nearly a third of the drink. Stirred, rocks with ice. Bitter orange, grape, and corn whiskey, and the cube.

History

The Boulevardier first appears in print in Harry MacElhone's 1927 Barflies and Cocktails, not in the main recipe list but in Arthur Moss's Cocktails Round Town essay, credited to Erskine Gwynne. Gwynne was an American in Paris who edited a monthly magazine of the same name from 1927 to 1932; Moss prints it as equal parts Campari, Italian vermouth, and bourbon. Ted Haigh notes that Americans had barely heard of Campari in 1927, and that the Negroni would not see print for another twenty years. This menu keeps the bourbon and the rocks glass, yet it pours an ounce and a half of whiskey against an ounce each of Campari and sweet vermouth.
