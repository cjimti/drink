Taste

Two ounces of dry vermouth, three-quarters lemon, half an ounce of simple, egg white. The foam is the texture; the vermouth is the spirit, so the sour is grape and lemon under a dry foam. Shaken, coupe. A short sour with a dry cap.

History

A drink called Dry Vermouth Sour does not appear under that name in the pre-1970 manuals, nor in Difford's, Punch, or Imbibe. The parent is the sour itself: spirit, citrus, and sugar, printed by Jerry Thomas in 1862, with egg white common in American sours by the 1940s. Vermouth as a base is older still; J. Haney's 1869 Steward and Barkeeper's Manual already has a Vermuth Cocktail, and Harry Johnson's 1882 manual dresses vermouth with gum, bitters, and maraschino. This menu puts two ounces of dry vermouth through the sour template: three-quarters lemon, half an ounce of simple, egg white, coupe.
