Taste

Three-quarters each of gin, Scotch, white crème de cacao, and cream, so the two spirits are half the glass and the cream is a quarter of it. Shaken, coupe. Chocolate and smoke over a thin cream body, with juniper coming up behind the whisky.

History

The Barbary Coast is a Prohibition-era drink with no known author. Harry Craddock prints it in The Savoy Cocktail Book in 1930 as equal parts gin, Scotch, crème de cacao, and cream, and Crosby Gaige runs the same drink in his 1945 Cocktail Guide and Ladies' Companion under the name Death and Mr. Morgenthau. The name is San Francisco's: the nine blocks around Pacific Street that worked as a red-light district for sailors from the 1850s until the city shut it down. This menu pours three-quarters of each, shaken, coupe.
