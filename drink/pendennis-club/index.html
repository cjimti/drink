Gin, shaken

Pendennis Club

2,1,Q,2,cLw

  • 2 oz Gin
  • 1 oz Apricot liqueur

Anything with juice, egg, or syrup carrying the drink. Hard shake, double strain.

Coupe, lime wheel.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of gin against a full ounce of apricot liqueur, so the stone fruit is a third of the drink. Three-quarters lime is tart against the apricot; two dashes of Peychaud's pull cherry and anise through it. Shaken, coupe, lemon twist and a wheel. Pink, lime, and apricot.

History

The Pendennis Club in Louisville, founded in 1881, gives the drink its name. Two recipes print under it. William T. Boothby's 1908 World's Drinks lists gin, apricot brandy, and French vermouth, no lime. Charles H. Baker's 1939 Gentleman's Companion calls it The Pendennis Club's Famous Special: gin, dry apricot brandy, lime or lemon, two dashes of Peychaud's, strained over a split kumquat. The club later lost the running story of who mixed it. This menu is Baker's sour: two ounces of gin, one of apricot, three-quarters lime, two dashes of Peychaud's, shaken, with a lemon twist and a wheel and no kumquat.
