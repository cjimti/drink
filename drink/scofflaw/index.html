Rye, shaken

Scofflaw

Barline 1,1,h,h,1,co

  • 1 oz Rye
  • 1 oz Dry vermouth
  • 1/2 oz Grenadine
  • 1/2 oz Lemon juice
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Coupe, orange twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces of rye and dry vermouth, so the wine is half the drink. Half an ounce of grenadine, half of lemon, one dash of orange bitters. Shaken, coupe, orange twist. The rye is spice, the grenadine pomegranate, the lemon a sour cut through the dry vermouth.

History

Delcevare King, a Massachusetts teetotaler, ran a 1924 contest for a word to shame drinkers who flouted Prohibition; Henry Irving Shaw and Kate L. Butler split the prize for "scofflaw." The Chicago Tribune reported a Scofflaw cocktail at Maxim's in Paris on 21 January 1924: three parts rye, two parts French vermouth, a dash of lemon and of grenadine. Harry MacElhone claimed Jock at Harry's New York Bar invented it days later, with orange bitters and equal Canadian Club and French vermouth. Difford's notes the Tribune piece on Maxim's predates MacElhone's claim. This menu is one to one rye to dry vermouth, half an ounce each of grenadine and lemon, one dash of orange bitters, shaken, coupe, orange.
