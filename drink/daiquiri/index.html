Taste

Two ounces of light rum, three-quarters lime, half an ounce of simple. Lime leads; the sugar is only there to keep the rum from going thin. Shaken hard and strained into a coupe, no ice in the glass, no blender. Cane and pith, cold.

History

Jennings Stockton Cox, an American mining engineer at the Daiquiri iron mines near Santiago de Cuba, mixed Bacardi, lime, and sugar around 1898. Francesco Domenico Pagliuchi wrote in 1948 that he was there and suggested the name; Basil Woon's 1928 When It's Cocktail Time in Cuba records Cox naming it at Santiago's Venus bar. The first recipe-book printing is Jacques Straub's 1914 Drinks, misspelt Daiguiri. Constantino Ribalaigua Vert later numbered several Daiquiris at El Floridita in Havana; the frozen drink is his, not this one. This menu is two ounces of light rum, three-quarters lime, and half an ounce of simple, shaken, coupe.
