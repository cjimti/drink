Scotch, stirred

Affinity

1h,Q,Q,2,clc

  • 1 1/2 oz Scotch
  • 3/4 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 3/4 oz Dry vermouth
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, lemon twist, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of scotch, then three-quarters each of sweet and dry vermouth, so the whiskey leads and the vermouths split. Two dashes of Angostura sit under both. Stirred, coupe, lemon and cherry. A Perfect Manhattan on scotch: malt, grape, and bark.

History

The Affinity first appears in The New York Sun on 28 October 1907 as a Broadway cocktail of Scotch whisky, Italian vermouth, powdered sugar, and orange bitters. The version that stuck, equal French vermouth, Italian vermouth, and Scotch with aromatic bitters, prints in Jacob A. Didier's The Reminder in 1909, is repeated by Hugo Ensslin in 1917, and is the formula Harry Craddock puts in The Savoy Cocktail Book in 1930. This menu keeps both vermouths and two dashes of Angostura, yet it pours an ounce and a half of scotch against three-quarters of each vermouth, so the whiskey leads.
