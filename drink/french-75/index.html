Gin, shaken

French 75

Barline 1h,1,h,3,Rl

  • 1 1/2 oz Gin
  • 1 oz Lemon juice
  • 1/2 oz Simple syrup
  • 3 oz Champagne

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Rocks, iced, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of gin against a full ounce of lemon, so the citrus is a real pour, then a half ounce of simple and three ounces of Champagne. Shaken, then the wine. Rocks with ice and a lemon twist. The lemon stays loud over the bubbles; the cube melts into it.

History

The name is the French 75-millimetre field gun of the First World War. The first drink so called, reported by O. O. McIntyre in the Washington Herald in 1915, is gin, applejack, grenadine, and lemon; Robert Vermeire, in 1922, credits Henry Tépé of Henry's Bar in Paris. The gin, lemon, sugar, and Champagne formula first prints in Judge Jr's 1927 Here's How, in a tall glass: if you use club soda, you have a Tom Collins. Harry Craddock repeats it in the 1930 Savoy Cocktail Book, poured over cracked ice. The flute is later. This menu keeps the gin and the ice, yet it pours a full ounce of lemon and three of Champagne into a rocks glass with a lemon twist.
