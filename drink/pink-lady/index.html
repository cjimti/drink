Apple Brandy, shaken

Pink Lady

1h,Q,Q,h,cc

  • 1 1/2 oz Gin
  • 3/4 oz Apple brandy
  • 3/4 oz Lime juice
  • 1/2 oz Grenadine
  • Egg white

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Coupe, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

Gin at an ounce and a half, then three-quarters of apple brandy and three-quarters of lime, a half ounce of grenadine, and egg white. The foam is the texture; the apple is a real pour. Lime and gin keep it sharp. Grenadine is color and a little pomegranate. Coupe, cherry.

History

The Pink Lady takes its name from Ivan Caryll's 1911 Broadway musical, starring Hazel Dawn. Wondrich records that the drink mixed for her at Murray's Roman Gardens was ojen with Peychaud's and Angostura, not this one. Jacques Straub's 1913 Manual of Mixed Drinks prints the version that entered the books: equal gin, applejack, and lime, with dashes of grenadine, and no egg. Later recipes drop the apple brandy, add egg white or cream, or both. Craddock's Savoy omits the applejack and adds the white. This menu keeps Straub's gin, apple brandy, lime, and grenadine, and it shakes egg white with them.
