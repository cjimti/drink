Angel Face

1,1,1,c

  • 1 oz Gin
  • 1 oz Apple brandy
  • 1 oz Apricot liqueur

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces of gin, apple brandy, and apricot, three bottles at the same weight. Stirred, coupe. Juniper and orchard apple with apricot over the top, and since the apricot is a liqueur it is the sugar in the drink as well as the fruit. Three ounces of spirit and nothing to lengthen it, so it drinks warm and slow.

History

Harry Craddock prints the Angel Face in The Savoy Cocktail Book in 1930 as a third each of dry gin, apricot brandy, and Calvados, shaken and strained into a cocktail glass. His apricot brandy meant the drier eau-de-vie rather than the sweetened liqueur most bars reach for now, which is why the modern version reads sweeter than his did. The name gets attached to Abe Angel Face Kaminsky, who robbed Detroit speakeasies, though nothing ties him to the glass. This menu keeps the equal ounces and stirs it, since there is no juice, egg, or syrup carrying it, coupe.
