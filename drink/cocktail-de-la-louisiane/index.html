Rye, stirred

Cocktail de la Louisiane

Q,Q,Q,2b,3,clc

  • 3/4 oz Rye
  • 3/4 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 3/4 oz Bénédictine
  • 2 barspoons Absinthe
  • 3 dashes Peychaud's bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, lemon twist, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal three-quarters of rye, sweet vermouth, and Benedictine, so the herbal liqueur is a third of the drink. Two barspoons of absinthe is anise you can taste. Three dashes of Peychaud's. Stirred, coupe, lemon and cherry. Honey, wormwood, and rye.

History

Stanley Clisby Arthur prints Cocktail à la Louisiane in Famous New Orleans Drinks and How to Mix 'Em (1937) as the special cocktail of Restaurant de la Louisiane, a French Quarter Creole house opened in 1881: equal rye, Italian vermouth, and Benedictine, three or four dashes each of absinthe substitute and Peychaud's, strained onto a cherry. Whether it predates Walter Bergeron's Vieux Carré is disputed; both first see print in Arthur's book. This menu keeps Arthur's equal three-quarters, pours two barspoons of absinthe and three dashes of Peychaud's, coupe, lemon and cherry.
