Taste

An ounce and a half of dry vermouth, three-quarters of Bénédictine, three-quarters of lime. No base spirit. The liqueur is honey and herbs; the lime matches it, so the drink is tart as well as sweet. Shaken, coupe. Vermouth and Bénédictine, then pith.

History

Queen Elizabeth is a crowded name. The version here is the vermouth one: dry vermouth, Bénédictine, and lime, with no gin and no brandy. David Wondrich found the formula in a Bénédictine advertisement in the February 1935 Esquire, billed as the prize-winning recipe in a nationwide cocktail contest. Difford's Guide credits Herbert L. Quick, head bartender at the Benjamin Franklin Hotel in Philadelphia in 1934, who named it for his wife, not the monarch. Other Queen Elizabeths in the manuals are gin drinks, or brandy and sweet vermouth. This menu pours an ounce and a half of dry vermouth and three-quarters each of Bénédictine and lime.
