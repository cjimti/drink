Scotch, stirred

Bobby Burns

1h,1,h,cl

  • 1 1/2 oz Scotch
  • 1 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 1/2 oz Bénédictine

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of scotch against a full ounce of sweet vermouth, so the grape sits nearly even with the malt. Half an ounce of Bénédictine is honey and herbs, a pour not a dash, enough to coat the drink. Stirred, coupe, lemon twist. Scotch first, then the liqueur's spice.

History

The Bobby Burns is a Scotch Manhattan with Bénédictine. Harry Craddock prints it in The Savoy Cocktail Book in 1930 as equal Italian vermouth and Scotch whisky with three dashes of Bénédictine. Albert Stevens Crockett, a year later in Old Waldorf-Astoria Bar Days, lists a Robert Burns with absinthe and orange bitters, and says it may have been named for the poet or for a cigar salesman who bought at the old Waldorf bar. Earlier printings use Baby Burns (Bishop and Babcock, 1900) and Robert Burns (Grohusko, 1910). This menu keeps Craddock's scotch, sweet vermouth, and Bénédictine, yet it pours an ounce and a half of whiskey, a full ounce of vermouth, and a half ounce of the liqueur.
