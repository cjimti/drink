Aged Rum, shaken

Brass Rail

Barline 1h,h,1,h,2,c3

  • 1 1/2 oz Aged rum
  • 1/2 oz Bénédictine
  • 1 oz Lemon juice
  • 1/2 oz Simple syrup
  • 2 dashes Orange bitters
  • Egg white
  • 3 dashes Angostura bitters on top

Shake hard with ice, then strain.

Coupe, 3 dashes of bitters.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of aged rum, a half of Benedictine, a full ounce of lemon, a half of simple: a rum sour with a herbal liqueur in the sugar slot. Egg white gives it a foam cap. Two dashes of orange bitters sit in the shake; three dashes of Angostura are dropped on the foam, clove and bark on the white. Coupe.

History

Tony Abou-Ganim built the Brass Rail as a nightcap for his cousin Helen David, who favored Benedictine, and named it for her bar in Port Huron, Michigan, which she opened in 1937. Difford's Guide adapts the recipe from Abou-Ganim: aged rum, Benedictine, lemon, sugar, egg white, and orange bitters, with a flamed orange twist and cinnamon on the foam. This menu uses aged rum, Benedictine, a full ounce of lemon, half an ounce of simple, two dashes of orange bitters, egg white, and three dashes of Angostura dropped on the foam.
