Light Rum, built

Mojito

Barline 2,Q,h,t,Hm

  • 2 oz Light rum
  • 3/4 oz Lime juice
  • 1/2 oz Simple syrup
  • Mint
  • top with Soda water

Build in the glass over ice, then lift once with a barspoon.

Highball, iced, mint.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of light rum, three-quarters of lime, half an ounce of simple, mint muddled into the glass, then soda over ice. Bruised mint gives oil; shredded mint gives green. Cane, lime, and cold air. A sprig on top, slapped once to wake it up.

History

The ancestor is the Draque, cane spirit with lime, sugar, and mint, which Cubans were taking daily as medicine by the 1830s and which legend ties to Francis Drake in 1586. When Bacardi's rum replaced the rough aguardiente the drink got the new name. Printed recipes turn up as Mojo Criollo in 1927 and Mojo de Ron in 1929, and the name Mojito appears on the souvenir menu of Sloppy Joe's Bar in Havana for the 1931-32 season, alongside a gin version. La Bodeguita del Medio hangs a note signed by Hemingway claiming his Mojito; Difford's doubts it, since nothing Hemingway published mentions either the drink or the bar. This menu pours two ounces of light rum, three-quarters lime, half an ounce of simple, muddled mint, and soda over ice.
