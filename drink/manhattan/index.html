Bourbon, stirred

Manhattan

2,1,2,cc

  • 2 oz Bourbon
  • 1 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of bourbon to one of sweet vermouth, so the whiskey leads. Two dashes of Angostura sit under the vermouth's grape and spice. Stirred, coupe, cherry. Corn, bark, and the cherry at the bottom.

History

The Manhattan is American whiskey, Italian vermouth, and bitters, first named in print in an 1882 Gotham Gossip column syndicated out of New York, and first given a full recipe in O.H. Byron's 1884 The Modern Bartenders' Guide. The Manhattan Club dinner for Samuel Tilden in 1874, later tied to Jennie Jerome, is a late story: she was in England with a month-old Winston Churchill. William F. Mulhall, writing in 1923, credited a man named Black who kept a place on Broadway; David Wondrich found George Black's Manhattan Inn near Houston Street in the 1870s. Early books call for rye or bourbon, often at equal parts. This menu files under bourbon, pours two to one, two dashes of Angostura, coupe, cherry.
