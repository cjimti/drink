Taste

Two ounces of gin against three-quarters of lime, so the lime is loud, then a half ounce of simple. Shaken, coupe. The lime is fresh and tart; the syrup is a thin coat of sugar, enough to take the edge. Juniper under citrus.

History

Lauchlan Rose of Edinburgh patented an alcohol-free lime cordial in 1867, the same year the Merchant Shipping Act required British ships to carry lime juice against scurvy. Officers mixing gin with Rose's is the navy story; a gimlet is also a barrel tap. The first named print is Harry MacElhone's 1922 Harry's ABC of Mixing Cocktails: equal parts Plymouth gin and Rose's, "a very popular beverage in the Navy." Rear-Admiral Sir Thomas Desmond Gimlette is sometimes credited; his Times obituary does not mention the drink. This menu pours two ounces of gin, three-quarters of fresh lime, and a half ounce of simple, coupe.
