Gin, stirred

Fitchett

Barline 1h,Q,q,1,cc

  • 1 1/2 oz Gin
  • 3/4 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 1/4 oz Bénédictine
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Coupe, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

An ounce and a half of gin against three-quarters of sweet vermouth, then a quarter ounce of Bénédictine for honey and herbs. One dash of orange bitters. Stirred, coupe, cherry. Gin first, grape, then a thin herbal coat. Wetter and less herbal than the Vancouver on this menu.

History

The Fitchett prints in the same About Town Cocktail Book as the Vancouver, around 1925. The unnamed writer credits Joseph A. Fitchett, head bar steward of the Vancouver Club: of the thousand and one drinks he can mix, it is the best. The formula is fifty percent gin, thirty percent Italian vermouth, twenty percent Bénédictine, a dash of orange bitters, olive or cherry. It is the sweet-vermouth sibling of the Vancouver in that book. This menu keeps the gin and sweet vermouth, yet it pours only a quarter ounce of Bénédictine, coupe, cherry.
