Rye, stirred

Saratoga

1,1,1,2,cl

  • 1 oz Rye
  • 1 oz Cognac
  • 1 oz Sweet vermouth
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Coupe, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces of rye, cognac, and sweet vermouth, so neither spirit leads. The cognac rounds the rye; two dashes of Angostura sit under grape and barrel. Stirred, coupe, lemon. A Manhattan that split the whiskey with brandy.

History

David Wondrich, in The Oxford Companion, treats the Saratoga as two competing drinks that shared the name of the New York spa town. Jerry Thomas's posthumous 1887 Bar-Tenders Guide prints equal ponies of brandy, whiskey, and vermouth with two dashes of Angostura, shaken, in a claret glass with a quarter slice of lemon. Harry Johnson, the following year, printed a fancy brandy cocktail with a squirt of champagne. For thirty years the books took sides; by the 1930s both were a memory. This menu is Thomas's split-base Manhattan: one ounce each of rye, cognac, and sweet vermouth, two dashes of Angostura, coupe, lemon.
