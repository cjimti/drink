Taste

Two ounces of gin, three-quarters of dry vermouth, a quarter ounce of orange liqueur, two dashes of orange bitters. The vermouth still leads the dressing; the liqueur is peel and a little sugar, not a third of the drink. Stirred, coupe. Drier than this menu's Martini, with candied orange instead of a twist.

History

Wayne Collins built the Dry Orange Martini in 2003 for Maxxium UK: two ounces of gin, three-quarters of dry vermouth, a quarter of orange liqueur, two dashes of orange bitters. Collins was a London bartender who ran Mixxit, Maxxium's training programme, and later put the White Negroni into wider print. The parent is the Dry Martini; Harry Johnson's 1888 Martini already carried orange curaçao, which later books dropped as the drink dried out. This menu keeps Collins's measures and the coupe.
