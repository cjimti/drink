Taste

An ounce and a half of sherry to three-quarters of sweet vermouth, two fortified wines and nothing stronger, so it pours low. Two dashes of orange bitters. Stirred, coupe, orange twist. Nutty dry wine under the vermouth's grape and sugar, light enough to drink before dinner.

History

The Adonis is named for William Gill's Adonis, a musical burlesque that opened in Chicago in the summer of 1884 and moved to the Bijou Theatre in New York that September with Henry E. Dixey in the title role. The Waldorf is usually credited with the drink. The first print record is a Pearls from Billy, the Bartender column in the New York Sun on 27 March 1887, which puts the making of it around 1885 or 1886. It is the sweet half of the pair of nineteenth-century sherry aperitifs, the Bamboo being the dry one. This menu pours an ounce and a half of sherry, three-quarters of sweet vermouth, and two dashes of orange bitters, coupe, orange twist.
