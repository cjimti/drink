Tequila, built

Paloma

2,1,h,h,t,HS

  • 2 oz Tequila
  • 1 oz Grapefruit juice
  • 1/2 oz Lime juice
  • 1/2 oz Simple syrup
  • top with Soda water

Made in the glass it is served in. Ice, spirit, then the mixer on top; one lift with a barspoon.

Highball, iced, salt rim.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of tequila, a full ounce of grapefruit, half an ounce each of lime and simple, then soda over ice. Building the grapefruit half out of juice, syrup, and plain soda leaves the agave in front; a grapefruit soda would sit over it. Salt on the rim pulls the bitter edge off the grapefruit.

History

The paper trail starts with a 1950 brochure for Squirt, an American grapefruit soda, suggesting the mix with tequila. Squirt launched in Mexico in 1955 and advertised hard there in the early 1970s, and who first adapted it to Mexican taste is unknown. It was a Jalisco drink through the 1970s and 1980s, associated with the bars on the Parian square in Tlaquepaque, reached Southern California by the late 1990s, and spread across the United States in the early 2000s. Paloma is Spanish for dove. This menu builds the soda half from an ounce of fresh grapefruit, half an ounce of lime, half an ounce of simple, and plain soda water, over ice with a salt rim.
