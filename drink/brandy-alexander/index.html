Taste

Equal ounces of cognac, crème de cacao, and cream, so chocolate and dairy are two thirds of the drink. Shaken, coupe. Grape brandy, cocoa, and a short cream sour with no citrus.

History

The Alexander starts as a gin drink in Hugo Ensslin's 1916 Recipes for Mixed Drinks: equal gin, crème de cacao, and cream. The brandy version took over in the 1920s and 1930s; Harry Craddock prints a Brandy Alexander in the 1930 Savoy. It became the default Alexander. This menu keeps the equal ounces of cognac, cacao, and cream, shaken, coupe, no dusting of nutmeg.
