Cognac, stirred

Metropole

Barline 1h,1h,2,1,cc

  • 1 1/2 oz Cognac
  • 1 1/2 oz Dry vermouth
  • 2 dashes Peychaud's bitters
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Coupe, cherry.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces and a half of cognac and dry vermouth, so neither wins. Two dashes of Peychaud's pull it toward cherry and anise. One dash of orange bitters is peel on the dry vermouth. Stirred, coupe, cherry. It reads pink and lean.

History

George J. Kappeler prints the Metropole in 1895 Modern American Drinks as the house cocktail of the Hotel Metropole at Broadway and 42nd Street: equal brandy and French vermouth, gum, Peychaud's, orange bitters, and a cherry. It is a dry, Peychaud's turn on O.H. Byron's 1884 Metropolitan. Albert Stevens Crockett, in The Old Waldorf Bar Days (1931), calls the hotel somewhat lively. The street-level Cafe Metropole held an all-night license; Herman Rosenthal was shot outside it in 1912, and the hotel went bankrupt that July. This menu keeps Kappeler's equal parts, two dashes of Peychaud's, one of orange bitters, and the cherry. No gum.
