Gin, stirred

Martini

2,1,1,cl

  • 2 oz Gin
  • 1 oz Dry vermouth
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of gin to one of dry vermouth, so the wine is a third of the drink, not a rinse. One dash of orange bitters is peel in the glass. Stirred, coupe, lemon twist. It drinks wet and cold: juniper, then the vermouth's wormwood and grape, then a thin orange film.

History

The first gin-and-vermouth drinks print in 1883 newspaper copy from Cleveland and Chicago, still called Manhattans, then as Martinez in O.H. Byron's 1884 The Modern Bartender's Guide and as Martini in Harry Johnson's 1888 Bartenders' Manual: Old Tom, sweet vermouth, curaçao, gum, and bitters. The Dry Martini, French vermouth and a drier gin, is named in New York by 1896 and is the dominant version by 1900. Two-to-one gin to vermouth appears in the late 1890s and is standard by the 1910s; orange bitters stay until the 1940s, then drop as the ratio marches past five-to-one. This menu keeps the wet 2:1, one dash of orange bitters, coupe, lemon.
