Mint Julep

2,q,Rm

  • 2 oz Bourbon

  • Mint

Build in the glass over ice, then lift once with a barspoon.

Rocks, iced, mint.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of bourbon and a quarter ounce of simple over ice, with mint muddled into the glass. A quarter of syrup against a full pour of whiskey is a light hand, so the drink is bourbon and mint oil, cold. It frosts the glass while it sits and it keeps diluting as it goes.

History

The word comes through the Arabic julab, rosewater, and the drink through Persia and Europe, where mint took the place of rose petals. John Davis, in Travels in the United States (1803), defines a julep as a dram of spirituous liquor that has mint steeped in it, taken by Virginians of a morning. Captain Frederick Marryat carried it back to Britain in 1839 and called it one of the most delightful and insinuating potations that ever was invented. Brandy and rum gave way to whiskey by 1900, and Churchill Downs made it the official drink of the Kentucky Derby in 1938. This menu pours two ounces of bourbon, a quarter ounce of simple, and muddled mint over ice in a rocks glass rather than a silver cup.
