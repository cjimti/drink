Air Mail

  • 1 1/2 oz Aged rum

Anything with juice, egg, or syrup carrying the drink. Hard shake, double strain.

Rocks, iced.

Open in the menu

Taste

A rum sour of an ounce and a half of aged rum, three-quarters lime, three-quarters honey, then two ounces of Champagne over ice in a rocks glass. Honey is thicker and waxier than sugar, so the rum tastes darker than a Daiquiri. The bubbles and the melt are the lift.

History

The Air Mail first saw print in Bacardi and Its Many Uses, a 1930 pamphlet from the still-Cuban company. Jeff Berry, writing in The Oxford Companion to Spirits and Cocktails, notes that Cuba began air-mail service that year, yet the pamphlet names no bartender. W.C. Whitfield's 1941 Here's How prints it as rum, lime, honey, and Champagne in a highball, with the line "It ought to make you fly high." Some later writers treat it as a rum French 75. The early printings, however, are a honey Daiquiri topped with sparkling wine and served long, over ice. This menu keeps the honey and the Champagne, uses aged rum, and pours it in a rocks glass with ice.
