Vermouth, stirred

Bamboo

1h,1h,d,2,cOl

  • 1 1/2 oz Sherry
  • 1 1/2 oz Dry vermouth
  • 1 dash Angostura bitters
  • 2 dashes Orange bitters

Spirit only. Stir on ice until cold, strain, no dilution past that.

Coupe, olive, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces and a half of dry sherry and dry vermouth, so there is no base spirit. One dash of Angostura, two of orange bitters. Stirred, coupe, olive and lemon twist. Nutty, dry wine, a little bark.

History

Louis Eppinger is credited with the Bamboo at the Grand Hotel in Yokohama, sometime in the 1890s: equal sherry and dry vermouth, bitters, a twist. It appears in American books by 1908. David Wondrich, in Imbibe, treats it as the low-proof cousin of the Martini, sherry in the gin slot. This menu pours equal ounces and a half, one dash of Angostura and two of orange bitters, coupe, olive and lemon.
