Gin, stirred

Negroni

Barline 1,1,1,R

  • 1 oz Gin
  • 1 oz Campari
  • 1 oz Sweet vermouth

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Rocks, iced.

Open in the menu

Taste

Equal ounces of gin, Campari, and sweet vermouth. Campari is a third of the drink: bitter orange against grape and juniper. Stirred, rocks with ice, no twist on the menu. The cube is the dilution, so the Campari stays loud as it sits.

History

The usual story puts Count Camillo Negroni at Caffe Casoni on Via de' Tornabuoni in Florence around 1919, asking Fosco Scarselli to stiffen his Americano with gin in place of soda. Luca Picchi reconstructed that account in 2002; it is the better-supported one. However, Colonel Hector Andres Negroni later said there was no Camillo in the family tree and pointed instead to General Pascal Olivier Comte de Negroni. The equal-parts formula does not see print until Amedeo Gandiglio's 1947 Cocktails Portfolio. This menu pours one ounce each of gin, Campari, and sweet vermouth, over ice, no garnish letter.
