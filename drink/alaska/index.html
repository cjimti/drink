Gin, stirred

Alaska

2,h,1,cl

  • 2 oz Gin
  • 1/2 oz Chartreuse
  • 1 dash Orange bitters

Stir with ice until cold, then strain.

Coupe, lemon twist.

Open in the menu

Taste

Two ounces of gin to a half ounce of yellow Chartreuse, so the liqueur is a fifth of the pour: honey, dried herbs, and a faint saffron cast in the glass. One dash of orange bitters. Stirred, coupe, lemon twist. Juniper first, sweetness second, and an herbal finish that outlasts both.

History

The Alaska first appears in Jacques Straub's Manual of Mixed Drinks in 1913, made with Old Tom gin, yellow Chartreuse, and orange bitters. Harry Craddock prints it in The Savoy Cocktail Book in 1930 with more gin and less Chartreuse, and adds a note: so far as can be ascertained this delectable potion is NOT the staple diet of the Esquimaux, and it was probably first thought of in South Carolina, hence its name. David Embury, in 1948, records it as sometimes called the Oriental and prefers it drier still. This menu pours two ounces of gin, a half ounce of yellow Chartreuse, and one dash of orange bitters, coupe, lemon twist.
