Taste

An ounce and a half each of gin and dry vermouth, one dash of orange bitters. Half the drink is wine, so it is softer and lower proof than the Martini and the vermouth's grape and wormwood share the front with the juniper. Stirred, coupe, an olive. Salt from the olive against the wine.

History

Equal parts was the Martini ratio before Prohibition. Hugo Ensslin prints the Fifty-Fifty in 1917 in Recipes for Mixed Drinks, and Harry Craddock repeats it in the 1930 Savoy Cocktail Book: half gin, half French vermouth. The dry decades pushed the vermouth down to a rinse, and the ratio came back in New York in the late 1990s, at Sasha Petraske's Milk & Honey and then as Audrey Saunders' Fitty-Fitty at the Pegu Club in 2005, with orange bitters. This menu pours an ounce and a half each of gin and dry vermouth and one dash of orange bitters, stirred, coupe, olive.
