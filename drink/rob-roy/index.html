Taste

Two ounces of scotch to one of sweet vermouth, the Manhattan ratio. Two dashes of orange bitters, not Angostura, so peel rather than bark sits under the malt. Stirred, coupe, no garnish on the menu. Leaner than a bourbon Manhattan, orange oil in the grape.

History

The Rob Roy is a Scotch Manhattan, named for Reginald De Koven and Harry B. Smith's 1894 Broadway operetta about the Highland outlaw Robert Roy MacGregor. The Waldorf-Astoria, then on Fifth Avenue near Herald Square Theatre, is the usual credit; other writers point to the Fifth Avenue Hotel. David Wondrich found Henry A. Orphal at Duke's House in Hoboken, around 1895, swapping Scotch into a Manhattan for a whisky salesman who would not drink anything else. Early books often use orange bitters; later ones shift to Angostura. This menu keeps two ounces of scotch, one of sweet vermouth, and two dashes of orange bitters, coupe.
